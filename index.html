Who I Am

About Me

I’m a Data and Business Intelligence Analyst with more than three years of experience across business analysis, data management, operational reporting, and higher-education technology. I work across the analytical lifecycle—from understanding business questions and preparing reliable data to developing dashboards, reports, and analyses that help stakeholders make better decisions.

My experience includes cleaning, validating, reconciling, analyzing, and presenting data across operational, research, technology, and business environments. I have maintained 99%+ data accuracy in research datasets, analyzed datasets containing more than 500,000 records, developed KPI-driven reporting, and built analytical solutions using SQL, Power BI, Tableau, Excel, Python, and R .

Professionally, I have worked with several sides of analytics. As a Data Manager at Truman State University, I applied structured data-quality processes involving cleaning, validation, reconciliation, and quality control. In university IT, I supported users, maintained technology data, documented recurring issues, and developed a practical understanding of how technical systems, data, and people interact. In my current Business Analyst role, I analyze occupancy, revenue, reservations, booking patterns, and operational performance to support pricing, staffing, resource allocation, and daily business decisions.

I have also expanded my technical foundation through hands-on work with relational databases, data modeling, ETL/ELT, Azure Data Factory, Azure Databricks, PySpark, Apache Spark, and cloud-based data workflows. My project work spans business intelligence dashboards, SQL-based exploratory analysis, higher-education survey analytics, large-scale data analysis, and interactive applications built with R Shiny.

What I enjoy most is the problem-solving side of analytics: understanding what someone is really trying to learn from the data, determining the right analytical approach, building a trustworthy solution, validating the output carefully, and communicating the result in a way that technical and non-technical stakeholders can actually use.
